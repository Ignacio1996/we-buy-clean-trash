## Tia's full website review (May 12, 3:45 PM)

### Overall

> Your website has a strong core idea and a surprisingly clear value proposition compared to most recycling startups. The headline "We pay you for your trash" is immediately understandable, and the site has good momentum and visual pacing. The biggest opportunity now is not design — it's trust, clarity, and reducing skepticism.

A first-time visitor is likely thinking:
- "Is this real?"
- "How do they make money?"
- "What's the catch?"
- "Will this actually be worth my time?"
- "Can I trust them to come every week?"
- "Why are they paying in gift cards instead of cash?"
- "Who are these people?"

### What's already strong

1. **Clear core message.** "We pay you for your trash" is simple, memorable, and instantly understandable. Follow-up explanation ("Bag your aluminum, PET, glass, and cardboard. We pick it up at the curb every week and pay you in gift cards.") also works well.
2. **Strong "How It Works" flow.** The 3-step process (Order bags → Fill & set out → Return & redeem) is easy to follow and reduces friction.
3. **Specific numbers build credibility.** $840k paid, 3.4M lbs recycled, contamination reduced to 1.8% — strongest trust-building elements on the site.

### Biggest problems (trust + credibility)

1. **The site feels "too perfect."** The live feed ("4 seconds ago", "1,284 pickups today", constant updates) feels manufactured. Names are abbreviated, no photos, no proof, numbers change rapidly — it resembles crypto dashboards or gambling apps.
   - **Recommendation:** Replace the live ticker with real customer photos, real pickup photos, receipts, screenshots of payouts, testimonials with faces, "This week in Oakland we recycled…". The ticker can stay but should not dominate trust-building.
2. **No human presence.** No founder story, team photos, warehouse/truck/facility photos, or operational proof. The company feels anonymous.
3. **"Internal sort report · Q1 2026" weakens trust.** "Internal" sounds unverifiable.
   - **Better:** Partner with a city, publish a PDF, cite a third-party recycler, mention auditing, or phrase as "Based on 220,000 bags processed in Q1 2026."
4. **Gift cards instead of cash feels suspicious.** Users will ask: "Why not cash? Are points fake? Can I really redeem them?" The explanation comes too late.
   - **Recommendation:** Add near the hero: "Redeem anytime for Amazon, Walmart, Visa, Target, and 40+ brands." Show screenshots of redemption UI and a sample email redemption.
5. **Paying for bags creates friction.** "$8 per sheet of 10 bags" stops people immediately — "Wait… I have to pay first?" This is likely the largest conversion killer.
   - **Options:** First 10 bags free, starter kit refunded after first pickup, or first bag free.

### What's unclear

1. **What makes the bags special?** "Bag-tagging" is mentioned but never explained — why normal bags won't work, how bags are tracked, why contamination matters, what the tags do. Needs a visual: photo of bags, QR code example, "Every bag has a unique ID," diagram of the workflow.
2. **Pickup process.** "Tap set out for pickup" — but is there an app, SMS, email, or website dashboard? Unclear.
3. **Service reliability.** What if they don't pick up? What day? What time? What happens if it rains? Add operational reassurance.
4. **Is this legal / city approved?** Partnered with the city? Replacing municipal recycling? Allowed? Independent? Even one sentence helps.

### What to add immediately

1. **Real testimonials** — full names, faces, neighborhoods, real earnings, photos of bags/pickups. Example: *"We earned $18 our first month just from sparkling water cans." — Sarah M., Oakland.*
2. **"Seen In" section** — logos of local newspapers, sustainability blogs, recycling associations, city partnerships.
3. **Operational photos** — trucks, sorting, warehouse, scales, baled aluminum, drivers, pickups. Right now users cannot visualize the business actually existing.
4. **Trust indicators** — LLC/PBC registration, business address, phone number, support email, response times, terms summary, privacy summary. Footer is too minimal right now.

### Messaging improvements

- Current: *"We Buy Clean Trash."* — good brand name, but many users won't understand immediately.
- Stronger subheadline: *"Turn clean recyclables into weekly rewards with curbside pickup."* or *"We pay households for clean recycling pickups."*

### Biggest conversion improvement

Add a section called **"Why We Can Pay You"** — the real psychological question. Explain:
- traditional recycling contamination
- commodity pricing
- clean streams are valuable
- your system reduces contamination
- residents share in the upside

> This is partially explained in the FAQ, but it should be much earlier. That's your moat. That's the business model. That's the credibility engine. Right now it's buried.

### Design recommendation

The site currently feels modern, startup-y, clean, almost futuristic. For this business category, it needs slightly more:
- industrial realism
- operational transparency
- human warmth

> Too much polish without proof can feel fake.

### Overall assessment

**Strengths:** Excellent core concept · Clear onboarding flow · Strong headline · Fast understanding · Good use of numbers · Modern design language.

**Weaknesses:** Not enough proof · Feels anonymous · Operational legitimacy unclear · Live activity feed may reduce trust · Paying for bags creates friction · No human storytelling.

### Highest-ROI changes (priority order)

**Immediate**
1. Add founder/team section
2. Add real customer testimonials
3. Add photos/videos of operations
4. Explain "Why we can pay you"
5. Clarify redemption process visually

**Next**
6. Offer free starter bags
7. Add city/partner credibility
8. Add pickup reliability guarantees
9. Add operational metrics with proof

**Later**
10. Add case studies
11. Add neighborhood leaderboard/community stats
12. Add environmental impact certificates
13. Add ACH/cash payout option
