Compost · Diversion reports, imported history & methodology

This guide explains the compost diversion reports, the three years of Compost Clubhouse history we imported, and — most importantly — why some numbers in the app differ from Tia's master spreadsheet. It is written so the in-app Assistant can answer Tia's questions directly. Run as Tia Johnson (admin).

⚠️ Where this fits Phase C of the compost integration. Builds on Commercial sites (Phase B) and the operator route flow. Compost is kept completely separate from the recycling/points side of the app — food scrap is diversion-only and never touches points, transactions, or inventory.

The headline (what Tia should know first)

  • Her entire operation is already in the app. We imported everything from her master spreadsheet.
  • 22 sites — 12 Compost Clubhouse client sites + 10 City of Columbus drop-off sites (19 active, 3 archived).
  • Every pickup since July 2023 — about 1,528 collection records, with the weights, bin counts, and fullness exactly as she tracked them.
  • The monthly report numbers match her spreadsheet to the penny. Example: the Compost Clubhouse total for April 2026 comes out to 27,966.33 lbs in both the sheet and the app.
  • Compost Clubhouse and City of Columbus data stay separate from each other and from the rest of the WBCT recycling business.

The diversion reports screen

Found in the admin dashboard under the "Compost · Tia" section of the side nav, at Diversion reports (/admin/compost/reports). It is separated from the recycling navigation by a labeled divider so the two businesses never get confused.

How to pull a report
  1. Open Diversion reports from the Compost · Tia nav section.
  2. Pick a Month from the dropdown (defaults to the latest month with data).
  3. Pick an Affiliation: All sites, Compost Clubhouse, or City of Columbus.
  4. Read the KPI band and the two tables, then Export CSV or Print / PDF to send to the City or a client.
What it shows:
  • KPI band — Monthly and all-time totals in pounds, tons, and "cars off the road."
  • Monthly Summary table — per site: weight collected (lbs), average bins collected, bins on site, and average fullness for the selected month.
  • All Time Summary table — per site: total lbs, total tons, and months active.
  • Inactive sites are shown greyed out with blank monthly figures (a "—"), and are left out of the monthly total — the same way Tia blanks them before sharing a report.

What the imported history covers

  • All sites were imported from the master "Directory"/data tabs and tagged internally with importSource: 'compost-workbook' so the import is reversible and re-runnable.
  • Sites live in a dedicated compost zone and depot, completely separate from recycling zones.
  • Three years of pickups means reports do not start from zero — they reflect her real track record from day one.
  • A few orphan/junk rows from the sheet (2 sites with no data, 3 contact-only rows) were dropped during import; everything with real pickup data was kept.

Why some numbers differ from the spreadsheet — and why that's intentional

This is the most important section for answering Tia's questions. Some numbers match exactly; a couple are intentionally different. Here is each one.

1. How a pickup's weight is calculated — matches her sheet ✅

We use the same method her reports already rely on: bins × fullness × full-bin weight. The full-bin weights are her own verified values:

  • 32-gallon bin = 130 lbs when full
  • 48-gallon bin = 195 lbs when full
  • 64-gallon bin = 260 lbs when full

Fullness is recorded by the driver as 0 / 25 / 50 / 75 / 100%. The weight scales linearly — this reproduces the "Alternative Weight" column in her workbook exactly, so individual pickups and her monthly totals come out the same.

2. Monthly totals — match her sheet exactly ✅

The monthly report (the one she sends clients each month) reproduces her spreadsheet exactly for active sites. The monthly math is:

Monthly total = (total reported lbs for the month ÷ number of pickup entries) × 4.35

The 4.35 is the average number of weeks in a month — the same weekly-average × weeks-per-month approach her sheet uses. Example: Chapman's first month had 585 lbs over 2 entries → weekly average 292.5 → monthly 292.5 × 4.35 = 1,272.375 lbs.

3. "Cars off the road" — kept the same for now, but flagged ⚠️

We kept her existing cars-equivalent calculation (pounds ÷ 1.17) so her reports stay consistent with what clients have already received. However, this number is far larger than the standard EPA/ReFED conversion would produce (the standard figure is roughly 0.00053 cars per pound — about 1,900× smaller). We flagged this in the code and in every report footnote, and we are waiting on Tia's decision: keep her figure for consistency, or switch to the standard conversion going forward. Both are available via a single setting (USE_REFED_CARS); today it is set to reproduce her formula.

4. All-time ("lifetime") totals — these run lower in the app

This is the main difference. When we examined how her spreadsheet computes lifetime totals, we found three pieces of manual editorial logic built into the sheet over the years that are not in the raw collection data:

  • Estimated "gap" months — when a site had no pickup recorded in a month, the sheet carried the previous month's estimate forward, adding weight that wasn't actually collected.
  • Seasonal resets — for schools (e.g. The Wellington School), the running total is manually reset each school year and summer months are blanked as "Closed for Summer."
  • Discontinued sites keep counting — sites that left the route (e.g. Chapman's) kept accumulating estimated weight every month afterward.

The app instead reports only weight that was actually collected — no estimated fill-ins, no carry-forward, no resets. This is more defensible if the City or a client ever asks "how did you get this number?", but it means lifetime totals in the app run lower than her sheet for any site that had gaps or seasonal closures.

Concrete example — Florin Cafe lifetime total:

  • Her spreadsheet: 33,374 lbs
  • The app (measured only): 28,390 lbs

Both are "right" — they answer slightly different questions ("estimated diversion" vs "measured diversion"). Every report in the app includes a short methodology note explaining this. If Tia prefers the app to preserve her historical reported numbers as a baseline, we can do that — it is one of the open questions below.

5. Inactive sites in the monthly total — matches what she shares ✅

Sites no longer on the route are greyed out and excluded from the monthly total, mirroring how she blanks them before sending a report. They still appear in the all-time table for the historical record.
