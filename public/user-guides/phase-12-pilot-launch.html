Phase 12 · Pilot launch

The code is shipped — this phase is physical. Print stickers, hand-deliver the first bags, sign up ~5 neighbors in person, and watch them try the app. Everything below is a real-world checklist, not a test plan. Cross items off as you go; re-open the guide the next time you grow the pilot to another block.

Before day one

Buy the hardware

Rollo thermal printer — X1038 model or equivalent USB direct-thermal. $180-ish. You do not need the Rollo Ship cloud software; the app talks to it via the browser print dialog with a custom 2.25 × 1.25 page size.

Rollo-compatible labels — 2.25″×1.25″ direct thermal, 1000/roll. Buy two rolls; one resident chews through ~5 sheets per year.

Postal scale — a $20 digital kitchen scale is fine for pilot. Depot processing is manual weight entry; precision to the ounce is enough.

Bags — the physical recycling bag. Clear contractor bags (42-gal) are the pilot default; order 100 to start. Stickers apply to the bag, not to a box.

Lock the pilot footprint

The seed script (npm run seed:pilot) creates one zone (Kirk pilot, ZIP 80301, Wednesday pickup) and one depot (Kirk Depot). Before onboarding residents:

  1. Open /admin/zones and confirm the ZIP list actually matches the block you're piloting. If you're piloting a different ZIP, edit the zone in the admin UI (or edit scripts/seed-pilot.ts and re-seed).
  2. Confirm the depot address on the zone matches where you physically process bags (your garage, a friend's warehouse, etc.). This address appears on the compliance letters.
  3. Set pricing once at /admin/pricing. The seed defaults (aluminum $0.92, cardboard $0.08, etc.) are reasonable spring-2026 numbers but commodity prices move — check ScrapMonster the morning you launch.
Print enough stickers for week one

At /admin/bags, click Generate new sheet five times. Each sheet = 10 stickers = one resident's starter set. Five residents → five sheets. Print each sheet (Print + mark) and keep them labeled by sheet number in a folder.

Don't print all 50 at once on the first run — a jammed roll or misaligned template can eat a bunch of label stock. Print one, inspect the output (QR scans cleanly? number legible?), then print the rest.

Day one — onboarding the first 5 residents

Pre-walk the block

Before knocking, have Tia generate Initial Service Notice compliance letters for the zone at /admin/compliance and drop them in the recipients' mailboxes 2–3 days ahead. The letter explains the service and sets expectations so the doorstep conversation is shorter.

Legally the letter is what gets you past some municipalities' residential service rules — don't skip it even if you know the resident.

The doorstep pitch (~90 seconds)

Say this, roughly:

"Hi — I'm Nicolas, I sent the notice on Tuesday. We're running a weekly recycling pickup for this block. I take your separated recyclables on Wednesday, weigh them at my depot, and you get points worth ~30% of market value. No cost, no contract. I need 90 seconds to set you up and hand you your first bags — can I?"

Then, on your phone (or theirs):

  1. Open /signup on the resident's phone. Help them create an email + password (Google works too). Enter their address and phone.
  2. Wait for the welcome screen — they should see 10,000 welcome points (worth $1).
  3. Hand them one pre-printed sticker sheet plus 10 clear bags. Write their last name on the sheet with the marker (you reference this on delivery).
  4. Walk them through the Phase 3 loop on their phone: tap a sticker → open Scan Bag → declare sorted/mixed → take doorstep photo → submit. Do the first bag with them so they don't stall on Wednesday.
  5. Point at the Rewards tab so they know where points accumulate.

Set expectations out loud: "Bags out by 7am Wednesday. I'll text you when I'm on my way. Points land within an hour of pickup."

Mark the stickers claimed

You physically handed the sheet to the resident, but the app doesn't know yet — stickers are in the Printed · unassigned state until the operator (you) scans one at delivery. Two options:

  • Clean option: create a route for tomorrow with a single "deliver bag order" stop per resident, start the route in /operator, and scan the first sticker on each sheet to claim it. This is the production flow.
  • Pilot shortcut: use npm run dev:issue-bags -- <resident-email> from your laptop. This issues a sheet in a single command — faster for the first 5 residents but bypasses the operator flow, so you won't test it until week two.

Pick one and use it consistently — mixing the two makes the sheet numbers hard to reconcile.

Week one — first pickup cycle

Tuesday evening

Build tomorrow's route at /admin/routes:

  1. Click New route, pick the zone + tomorrow's date.
  2. Click Optimize order. The route runs through Google Maps and returns a waypoint order starting from the depot.
  3. Assign the operator (yourself). Click Save.
  4. Text the residents manually the first week: "Pickup tomorrow — bag out before 7am, sticker facing the street."
Wednesday morning

On your phone:

  1. Open /operator, tap today's route, hit Start route.
  2. Drive to the first stop. Tap Driver on the way if you want the SMS stub to fire (shows up in smsLog; real Twilio is still post-pilot).
  3. At the curb: scan the QR on the bag, confirm the declaration/photo match, tap Complete. If the bag is not out, tap Not out.
  4. After the last stop, tap Deliver to Depot. The route is now ready for processing.
Wednesday afternoon — processing

At the depot (garage), open /depot on a laptop:

  1. Incoming shows today's returned route. Tap into it.
  2. For each bag: scan the QR, weigh each of the 7 material piles on the scale, enter weights, pick contamination severity (be honest — even low levels), submit.
  3. Watch the resident get their SMS stub + points ledger entry. On their end, the Rewards page updates.

Aim for under 5 minutes per bag the first time. You'll get faster. Keep notes on paper for anything that felt awkward — that's Phase 13.

What to watch for

Signal What it means Fix
Resident can't find the Scan button Nav hierarchy or icon isn't obvious enough. Patch src/app/resident/page.tsx — probably surface Scan as a primary CTA.
QR scans fail in low light (porch at 7am) Camera auto-exposure / QR contrast. Increase QR error-correction level in /api/qr-sheet (currently M — try Q or H). Reprint affected sheets.
Resident forgets the bag Reminder cadence is wrong. Note frequency; next iteration wires up a Tuesday-evening SMS reminder (Twilio un-stub).
Weigh-in takes >5 min/bag The 7-material form is slow when the bag only has 2 material types. Future patch: collapse empty rows, add a "skip rest of form" shortcut.
Points feel abstract — resident asks "what's this worth?" Rewards page doesn't translate points-to-dollars prominently enough. Check src/app/resident/rewards/page.tsx; expand the conversion label.
Stripe/Twilio/gift cards stubbed out Known — pilot ships without payments + SMS + real rewards. Un-stub in this order post-pilot: Twilio SMS → Stripe bag orders → gift card fulfillment. Each lives behind its adapter file in src/lib/*.

End of week one — retro

Sit down Wednesday night and write:

  • Friction moments — each place a resident (or you) hesitated, fumbled, or had to be talked through something. Raw — one line each.
  • Data sanity — spot-check Firestore: do all 5 residents have a signup_bonus transaction plus a pickup transaction for this week? Does pointsBalance match the sum of their transactions? Any orphan docs?
  • Unit economics — total weight collected × customer price × customerPct. Subtract Rollo labels, bag cost, gas. Is the unit economics roughly sane at 5 residents? Extrapolate to 50.
  • Decision — grow the zone by another 5 next week, or tighten the existing flow first.

Growing past the first block

When the pilot zone is working, add residents, not zones. 20–30 residents in one Kirk zone stresses the app more than 5 residents across 3 zones. The real multi-zone work is separate — it touches route optimization, operator assignment, and the admin-per-zone compliance cadence, and it's deliberately out of scope until the single zone is stable.

When you do grow, re-run npm run seed:pilot is a no-op — use the admin UI to add zones/depots, not the seed script. The seed is only for bootstrapping empty state.
