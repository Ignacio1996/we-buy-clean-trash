Test Accounts & Ground Rules

Before you start testing, set up the accounts listed below. Every guide in this series refers to these same people by name, so you can follow along without getting lost about "who am I right now?"

The cast of testers

These are the sample accounts used throughout the guides. Real names and emails are up to you — swap in anything you like — but keep the roles the same, because the app behaves differently depending on which role is signed in.

Role Name we'll use Email What they do in the app
Admin Tia Johnson tia.admin@wbct-pilot.test Runs the back office. Sends invites, edits material pricing, creates zones and depots, builds pickup routes, approves gift card redemptions.
Resident Resident Aguirre resident.aguirre@example.com The primary homeowner tester. Signs up, orders bags, scans bags before pickup, checks her points, redeems a gift card.
Resident Resident Goode resident.goode@example.com A second resident used when a test needs two households on the same route (for example, verifying the route builder orders stops correctly).
Operator Operator Aguirre operator.aguirre@wbct-pilot.test The driver. Signs in on a phone, starts the day's route, scans each bag he picks up, delivers new bag sheets, closes out the route at the depot.
Depot Depot Goode depot.goode@wbct-pilot.test The sorter. Signs in at the depot (tablet or laptop), opens each bag Aguirre dropped off, weighs the seven materials separately, flags contamination, and submits — which awards points to the resident and adds weight to the depot inventory.
Tip — use separate browsers. Testing flows across two roles at once (e.g. Tia invites Aguirre, Aguirre accepts) is much easier if you open each account in a different browser or a private/incognito window. You can also use separate phones. Don't sign one account out just to sign another in — you'll lose your spot in the flow.

How to create these accounts

  1. Tia (admin) is created once, behind the scenes, by the dev team. You won't sign her up through the public website — she comes preloaded. Ask the dev team for her password.
  2. Aguirre and Goode (residents) sign themselves up on the public website. You'll walk through that in the Sign-up & sign-in guide.
  3. Aguirre (operator) cannot self-sign-up. Tia has to invite him by email from the admin dashboard first. You'll walk through that in the Admin dashboard guide before you get to operator testing.
  4. Goode (depot worker) also cannot self-sign-up. Tia invites her by email and also picks the depot she's assigned to — that link tells the app which inventory to update when Goode processes a bag. You'll do this when you get to the depot guide.
Why the asymmetry? Homeowners are the general public — anyone with a valid address in the pilot zone can sign up. Operators, depot staff, and managers are employees, so the system refuses to create those accounts unless an admin has first sent an invite. This prevents random people from giving themselves driver access.

What to have ready before you test

  • A laptop or desktop browser for Tia's admin work.
  • A second device (phone or a second browser window) for Aguirre the operator — operator screens are designed phone-first.
  • A tablet or laptop for Goode the depot worker — the process-bag screen has seven weight fields, so a real keyboard is easier than thumbs-on-glass.
  • A real address in the pilot zone for Aguirre and Goode. The zone is configured by the dev team; if your address isn't recognized during sign-up, tell them and they'll widen the zone for testing.
  • A test credit card number is not needed. Bag orders use a placeholder "pay" button during the pilot — no real money moves.

How to report what you find

Each guide has small empty checkboxes next to the things you should verify. As you work through a guide, tick what passes. If something looks wrong, jot a quick note — what you did, what you expected, what actually happened, and a screenshot if possible. Send the list back when you're done with the phase.

Don't worry about breaking things. This is a pilot build running against test data. You can create as many bags, orders, and routes as you want; nothing is going to real customers or real mail. If something seems stuck, reload the page. If it stays stuck, that's a bug — write it down.

What's stubbed in the pilot (not bugs)

A handful of features intentionally don't fully fire during the pilot. Each role guide repeats the subset that applies to it, but here's the full picture up front so you know what to expect:

⚠️ Features that look real but are simulated
  • Paying for bag orders — checkout ends on a mock "paid" screen. No card is charged.
  • Gift card redemption — the request lands in an admin queue; an admin emails the card manually. There's no automatic gift-card API fulfillment.
  • "Pay trash bill" and "Donate" redemption options are disabled placeholders.
  • SMS notifications (operator en-route, bag-processed receipt) are logged to the server console, not sent by text.
  • Invitation emails are logged to the console; copy the invite link from the server log to simulate the employee opening it.
  • Compliance notices — the app generates PDFs; an admin prints, stuffs, mails, and clicks Mark as mailed by hand.
  • Weighing bags is manual — no Bluetooth/USB scale integration.
  • Driver-on-the-way is tap-triggered by the operator, not GPS-geofenced.
