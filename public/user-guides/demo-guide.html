Client Demo Script

A 30–40 minute narrative walk-through of every feature, in the order that tells the story best. Follow it top-to-bottom and your client will see the whole system — resident, operator, depot, manager, admin — without any "now let me jump over here" whiplash.

How to use this doc. Each step has a click (▸) and, where useful, a short line you can read out (Say:). You're not reading it like a teleprompter — use it as a safety net so you don't forget to show a feature. Total run time is roughly 35 minutes at a comfortable pace; skip the optional bits if you need to hit 20.

Before the demo — 5-minute prep

  1. Open four browser profiles or windows and sign into each:
    • Resident Resident Aguirre — in your primary window (the one you'll screen-share).
    • Operator Operator Aguirre — on your phone, or a second window sized like a phone.
    • Depot Depot Goode — in a third window.
    • Admin Tia Johnson — in a fourth window; this is the "back office" you'll flip to last.
  2. As admin, pre-seed so the demo has something to show:
    • Resident Aguirre has a 10-bag sheet issued and at least one scanned bag queued.
    • A pickup route for today exists with Aguirre on it, assigned to Operator Aguirre.
    • Prices in /admin/pricing look current (no zeros).
    • There's at least one pending redemption in the queue so you can show the admin fulfilling it.
  3. Zoom your browser to ~110% so the client can read everything.
  4. Close extra tabs, silence notifications, and put your phone on Do Not Disturb.
  5. Have the full testing guide open in a spare tab in case the client asks "can I try it later?" — you'll send them that link.
If something breaks mid-demo. Don't panic and don't debug live. Say "Looks like a bug — I'll log it and show you the next piece." and move on. The script is modular; each act stands on its own.
Opening · 2 min

The pitch in 90 seconds

Before clicking anything, frame the product.

We Buy Clean Trash turns a household's recycling into cash-equivalent rewards. Residents sort into our bags, we pick up on a set day, and points land in their account based on the weight and market value of what they turned in. Points redeem for gift cards. What I'm about to show you is a working pilot — every screen is live, wired to a real database and real pricing.

Zoom to your landing page (/) and scroll top-to-bottom so they see the home experience before we sign in.

Act 1 · Resident onboarding · 5 min

A homeowner signs up

Stay in the resident window. Walk them through the front door of the app — what a real resident would see on day one.

Sign-up & the pre-signup AI scan

Open an incognito window side-by-side. Go to /scan.

Before a resident even creates an account, they can scan an item with their phone camera and our AI tells them roughly what it's worth. This is the hook — it's the first "wow" moment that gets someone to sign up.

Hold up a soda can, cardboard box, or plastic bottle. Capture it. Show the result — material, estimated weight, estimated reward.

Click Sign up or go to /signup.

Residents self-signup with any valid address inside our pilot zone. Employees — operators, depot, managers, admins — cannot sign up; they have to be invited by an admin. I'll show you the other side of that in a few minutes.

Either walk through the form to show it, or sign in as Resident Aguirre (who already exists).

Welcome & the onboarding walkthrough

If a brand-new account: land on /resident/welcome.

New residents get a quick walkthrough and a signup bonus — 10 points, the equivalent of ten cents — so their account isn't empty on day one.

Resident dashboard

Go to /resident.

This is home base for a resident. Their points balance, next pickup date, how many bags they have left, and quick links to order more bags or run the calculator.

Ordering bags

Click Order bags (/resident/order-bags).

Bags are sold in sheets of ten. They're compostable, labelled with a unique QR, and arrive on the next scheduled pickup. In the pilot we stub the payment step — in production this is a real Stripe checkout.

Place a test order so the client sees it enqueue onto a route.

The points calculator

Open /resident/calculator.

Residents who want to understand how they get paid can play with this: pick a material, enter a weight, see what it would earn. It reads the same live pricing the depot uses, so what you see here is exactly what you'd be awarded.

Enter 2 lb of aluminum. Then flip Declared separated on — point out the multiplier.

If the resident pre-sorts into a single-material bag and declares it, they earn 2×. That's the behaviour change we're designing for — separation at the source.

The in-app guide

Click Guide (/resident/guide).

Every resident screen has a help link — the guide is context-aware. It covers how to bag, what counts as contamination, pickup day rules.
Act 2 · Pickup day (resident side) · 2 min

Scanning a bag the night before pickup

Still as the resident, open /resident/scan-bag.

On pickup eve, the resident scans each bag they're putting out. Scanning tells us two things: which bags are going out, and whether it's a sorted single-material bag — which is what earns the 2× multiplier.

Scan a pre-printed QR (or type in the fallback number). Mark it as aluminum, separated.

What just happened under the hood. The bag is flagged as "scanned." Tomorrow the operator's route will list it, and the depot worker will see the declared material when they open it — so if it really is single- material and clean, they apply the 2× multiplier.

Behind the wheel of the pickup truck

Switch to the operator window. Sign in as Operator Aguirre if you aren't already.

Today's route

Land on /operator.

This is the driver's phone view. One button — Start the route — and then an ordered list of stops. The order is optimized with Google Maps Routes so the driver isn't thinking about directions, just executing.

Tap Start route.

Doing a stop

Tap the first stop to open /operator/stop/....

Each stop shows the resident's name, address, how many bags they scanned last night, and a "Driver on the way" button that sends them an SMS two stops before arrival.

Tap Driver on the way.

In the pilot that SMS is stubbed — we log it in a collection called smsLog so we can verify the content before wiring Twilio for real.

Scan the bags at the stop using the phone camera. Mark any missing bags. Hit Complete stop.

Delivering new bag sheets

If any stop has a pending bag order, the app flags /operator/deliver.

When a resident orders new bags, the driver's next route automatically carries them. The driver scans the sticker sheet onto the house — that's the handoff that ties physical bags to the resident account.

Closing out at the depot

Finish the last stop, then hit End route → /operator/summary.

End of day, the driver drops the collected bags at the depot. The summary screen confirms every bag handed off — chain of custody is intact.
Act 4 · Depot processing · 6 min

Where the bag becomes money

Switch to the depot window. Sign in as Depot Goode.

Incoming bags

Go to /depot/incoming.

Every bag the driver dropped off is sitting in this queue, grouped by resident. The depot worker opens one, pours it onto the sort table, and starts weighing.

Processing a bag — the moneyshot screen

Click a bag to open /depot/process/....

Seven weight fields, one per material — aluminum, tin and steel, cardboard, paper, PET, HDPE, mixed plastic. Each weight multiplies by today's market price for that material, times the resident's revenue share, and the total lands in the resident's account the instant this form submits.

Enter a few weights. Point at the running total that updates live.

Flip Contamination: Minor.

Contamination knocks points down by 30, 60, or 90 percent depending on severity. It's the stick that matches the separation 2× carrot — so people learn to sort properly.

Submit. Watch the success toast.

What just happened under the hood. One atomic write — the bagProcessing record, a new row in the resident's ledger, an updated points balance, and the depot's inventory all change together. You can never have "I got paid but the inventory is wrong" or vice versa.

Depot inventory

Go to /depot/inventory.

Running tally of every pound of every material currently on site. When we sell a load to a processor, the depot worker zeros that material out and logs the sale price — that's how we close the loop on actual realized revenue versus the price we paid residents against.

Act 7 · Admin back office · 10 min

The whole business on one dashboard

Switch to the admin window. Sign in as Tia Johnson.

Land on /admin.

This is mission control. Live inventory by material across every depot, zone-by-zone performance, contamination alerts, operator leaderboard. Everything else in the sidebar is one of the switches behind this dashboard.

Zones

Click Zones (/admin/zones).

A zone is a polygon on the map plus a pickup day. New service area? Draw a polygon, set the day, and residents with matching addresses can now sign up.

Pricing

Click Pricing (/admin/pricing).

Seven materials, each with a market price and the resident's revenue share. Commodity prices swing weekly, so the admin keeps these current. Every edit snapshots to a price history — so if a resident ever disputes how much they earned on a specific pickup, we know exactly what the price was that day.

Open aluminum, bump the price a penny, save.

Users & invites

Click Users (/admin/users).

Then Invites (/admin/invites).

Here's the asymmetric auth I mentioned. Residents self-signup; employees get invited. Tia picks a role — operator, depot worker, depot manager, or admin — adds an email, and the system sends a one-time invite link that encodes the role. No rogue drivers.

Send an invite to a throwaway email to demonstrate.

Route builder

Click Routes (/admin/routes).

Every morning Tia builds tomorrow's routes. Pick the zone, pick the date, and the app pulls every stop that needs service — scheduled pickups, bag deliveries, the works — and orders them with Google Maps. She picks an operator and hits publish. The driver sees it on their phone when they sign in.

Open an existing route, show the map and the ordered stops.

Bag sheets & QR printing

Click Bags (/admin/bags).

Every time a resident orders a sheet, ten QR stickers need to exist in the system. The admin generates a printable PDF here, feeds it through a Rollo thermal printer, and the physical stickers tie back to the same bag records the driver will scan. Every sticker has both a QR and a printed number as a fallback if the camera can't read it.

Redemptions queue

Click Redemptions (/admin/redemptions).

Remember the Amazon gift card the resident requested a few minutes ago? It's sitting here. Tia buys the gift card, pastes the code in, and the resident gets it — either by email or in-app. In production this becomes an automatic API call to Amazon's gift-card service.

Fulfill the one Resident Aguirre just requested. Flip back to the resident window and show it arrived.

Compliance

Click Compliance (/admin/compliance).

If a resident racks up repeated severe-contamination strikes, the system generates a mailed notice — certified-letter style. The app produces the PDF, Tia prints it and drops it in the mail, then clicks "marked as mailed" so the strike clock resets. That's the compliance loop: it's strict, but it's also fair and documented.
Closing · 3 min

Wrap, talking points, Q&A

Recap in one breath

We walked a bag from a resident's curb through pickup, through the depot, to a gift card. Every screen is live. Every number is calculated by code that's checked into a repo, not spreadsheets. This is ready to run a pilot with a real zone tomorrow.

Questions you'll probably get (and short answers)

  • "How do you handle payments for real?" — Stripe for bag orders, drop-in swap from the stub you saw. Gift cards auto-fulfilled through Amazon's Incentives API.
  • "What if the camera can't read the QR?" — Every sticker has a printed fallback number. The operator app has a manual-entry field for exactly that.
  • "Can we scale to multiple depots?" — Data model already has the fields. Today's UI is single-zone for the pilot; multi-zone is a UI change, not a rewrite.
  • "What happens if a driver calls out sick?" — Admin reassigns the route in the route builder with one dropdown change.
  • "How do you prevent people from gaming the 2× multiplier?" — The depot worker sees the declared material before they open the bag. If it's not what the resident said it was, they flag contamination and the multiplier doesn't apply.
  • "Where does the data live?" — Firestore (Google Cloud), backed up, access-controlled at the row level. No resident can read another resident's data.
  • "What's left to build?" — Real Stripe, real Twilio, real gift-card API, a few polish passes. Core operations run today.
